How Often Should Your Goodyear Property Get Landscape Service? A Practical Guide for HOAs, Offices, and Retail Sites

By ABC Landscaping LLC · Serving Goodyear and the West Valley, Arizona

When a property manager starts pricing out commercial landscape maintenance in Goodyear, AZ, one of the first things they ask is how often the crew needs to come out. It's a fair thing to ask, because frequency affects your budget more than almost anything else. But there's no single number that fits every property.

If the schedule is too tight, you pay for visits where the crew has very little to do. If it's too loose, problems pile up. Weeds settle into the rock beds, shrubs grow over walkways, and a leaking valve can run for two weeks before anyone notices. The right schedule sits somewhere between those two, and it depends on what's actually planted on your site.

A good provider of West Valley landscaping services should be able to walk your property and tell you why a schedule makes sense, not just hand you a package. To help you think it through before that walk, here's how frequency usually works for the property types we see most around Goodyear.

Why Service Frequency Matters So Much in the West Valley

Goodyear's climate is hard on landscapes. Summers regularly go past 110 degrees, monsoon storms roll through from July to September, and winter rain brings its own burst of growth. Plants here either grow very fast or barely move, depending on the season and the plant.

So the schedule that works in March may not be the one you need in July. A smart maintenance plan follows those seasonal changes instead of fighting them.

Property Type 1: HOA Communities With Lots of Turf

Usual fit: Weekly service

What these properties usually look like

Picture a typical Goodyear master-planned neighborhood: big greenbelts, a central park or playground, grass lining the main entry, and shrubs and trees spread through the common areas.

Why weekly service usually works

Bermuda grass loves the heat. In peak summer it can go from neat to ragged in four or five days, and once it gets ahead of you, catching up takes more work and leaves the turf looking scalped. Weekly mowing and edging keep the lawns even and the curb lines sharp.

There's another benefit people often overlook. When a crew is on site every week, they walk past the same sprinkler heads and turf areas again and again. A broken head or a dry spot gets caught within days, usually before a homeowner emails the board about it. That alone can save an HOA a lot of water and a lot of complaints.

Winter doesn't always slow things down either. If your community overseeds with ryegrass, that grass keeps growing through the cooler months and still needs regular mowing.

When the schedule might shift

Some HOAs split the property. The entrance, park, and high-visibility areas stay on weekly care all year, while back greenbelts and low-traffic common areas move to biweekly during winter. It's a reasonable way to save money without letting the parts residents see every day slip.

Property Type 2: Office Buildings With Desert Landscaping

Usual fit: Biweekly service

What these properties usually look like

Many office buildings in Goodyear use a low-water design: decomposed granite, desert shrubs, a handful of trees, drip irrigation, and little or no grass.

Why biweekly service usually works

With no lawn to mow, the weekly workload drops a lot. A visit every two weeks is usually enough to keep up with weed control, shrub shaping, blowing off walkways, and picking up litter.

The thing to watch with desert landscapes is weeds after rain. Both winter storms and summer monsoons can set off a flush of weeds in the granite almost overnight. On a two-week cycle, the crew pulls or treats them while they're still small, before they root deep and spread seed across the beds.

When the schedule might shift

If your building has a busy front entrance or you host clients and prospects regularly, think about adding a short mid-cycle visit just for the front. Your main approach stays clean without paying for full weekly service across the whole site.

Property Type 3: Small Retail Pads and Medical Offices

Usual fit: Biweekly or monthly service

What these properties usually look like

These are compact sites: a few parking lot islands, some planters by the door, maybe a strip along the street frontage, and not much plant material overall.

Choosing between biweekly and monthly

A small site with mostly mature, established desert plants can sometimes get by with monthly service. But retail stores and medical offices live on first impressions. A customer pulling into the lot notices litter in the islands, weeds coming through the rock, or a shrub blocking the monument sign, even if they couldn't tell you why the place felt a little run down.

For that reason, biweekly service is usually the safer choice for these properties. Monthly care tends to work best for sites set back from the road, with low foot traffic, or where the landscape is very simple.

Other Factors That Change How Often You Need Service

The property type gets you started, but a few other things can push your schedule one way or the other.

Season

Growth slows down a lot in the cooler months. Many Goodyear properties cut back their visit frequency from December through February, then go back to the regular schedule once spring growth starts.

Irrigation system

Spray irrigation has more moving parts than drip, which means more heads to break, more nozzles to clog, and more overspray to adjust. Properties on spray systems usually need more frequent checks than those running mostly on drip.

Trees and palms

Tree trimming and palm work generally run on their own schedule, separate from routine maintenance visits. Palms, for example, are often trimmed once a year after they flower, while other trees may need lifting or thinning on a different cycle.

Tenant and resident expectations

Sometimes the plants aren't what decides it. Class A office buildings and apartment communities focused on leasing often choose weekly service because the property has to look its best every day, even if the landscape could technically go longer between visits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I change my service frequency later?

Yes. Most commercial maintenance plans can be adjusted, and many properties change their schedule with the seasons. If your needs change, such as a new tenant, a landscape renovation, or an irrigation upgrade, the schedule can change with them.

Is biweekly service enough for an HOA?

For turf areas in summer, usually not. Bermuda grows too fast to stay tidy on a two-week cycle. Biweekly can work well for desert areas inside a community or for low-traffic sections during winter.

Does monthly service save money in the long run?

It can on simple, low-traffic sites. On busier properties, though, problems that build up between monthly visits often cost more to fix than more frequent service would have cost.
